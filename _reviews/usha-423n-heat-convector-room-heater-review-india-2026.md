## Cons

- Only suitable for small rooms of around 150 sq ft
- A fan heater runs its element continuously, so on long nights it can cost more in electricity than an oil-filled radiator that cycles on and off
- Fan heaters tend to dry out the air, which some people find uncomfortable overnight
- A tip-over safety switch is not listed for this model, so keep it on a flat, stable surface away from children and pets
- Only a 1-year warranty
- Some owners have asked for an auto-restart feature, which this model does not have

## FAQs

**Is the Usha 423N good for a bedroom?**
Yes, for a small bedroom of up to around 150 sq ft. For all-night use, run it on a lower setting with the thermostat, and keep the room slightly ventilated.

**How much electricity does the Usha 423N use?**
At full power it draws 2000W, or about 2 units of electricity per hour. On the lowest 665W setting it uses about a third of that. The thermostat can also reduce running time once the room is warm.

**Does the Usha 423N have a thermostat?**
Yes. It has an adjustable thermostat that helps keep the room at your chosen warmth instead of heating non-stop.

**Is it safe to use around children?**
It has a thermal cut-out, overheat protection and the ISI mark, but a tip-over switch is not listed for this model. Place it on a flat surface out of reach of children and pets.

**What is the warranty on the Usha 423N?**
Usha gives a 1-year warranty on this heater.
